Exam Practice 1 — Caesar (C) · Problem

No shift given — students must find it themselves.
Ciphertext
PBQROHFGREF YBIR PBQRF
  • Three words: 11 letters · 4 letters · 5 letters

Exam Practice 1 — Solution

Step 1 — count the first word
PBQROHFGREF = 11 letters. What common 11-letter phrase fits a Codebusters test? → CODEBUSTERS
Step 2 — find the shift from C→P
C = 2 (0-indexed A=0). Cipher P = 15. Shift = 15 − 2 = 13. Try ROT13.
Step 3 — verify all 11 letters (subtract 13 from each, wrap if negative)
P(15)−13=2=C   B(1)−13=14=O   Q(16)−13=3=D   R(17)−13=4=E   O(14)−13=1=B
H(7)−13=20=U   F(5)−13=18=S   G(6)−13=19=T   R(17)−13=4=E   E(4)−13=17=R   F(5)−13=18=S
C O D E B U S T E R S ✓
Step 4 — decode remaining words with shift 13
Y(24)−13=11=L   B(1)−13=14=O   I(8)−13=21=V   R(17)−13=4=E → LOVE
P(15)−13=2=C   B(1)−13=14=O   Q(16)−13=3=D   R(17)−13=4=E   F(5)−13=18=S → CODES
Answer: CODEBUSTERS LOVE CODES  ·  Shift = 13 (ROT13)
Teaching note: ROT13 is self-inverse — applying it twice returns the original. A great shortcut to remember.

Exam Practice 2 — Atbash (D) · Problem

No key needed — the reverse-alphabet mapping is always on the resource sheet.
Ciphertext
OVZIM GSV XRKSVI
  • Three words: 5 letters · 3 letters · 6 letters

Exam Practice 2 — Solution

Assumption: Atbash is self-inverse — decode = encode. Apply A↔Z, B↔Y, … to every letter.
Trick: plain + cipher letter positions sum to 25 (0-indexed). Use this to spot-check.
Word 1 — OVZIM
O(14)→25−14=11=L   V(21)→4=E   Z(25)→0=A   I(8)→17=R   M(12)→13=N
L E A R N ✓
Word 2 — GSV
G(6)→19=T   S(18)→7=H   V(21)→4=E
T H E ✓
Word 3 — XRKSVI
X(23)→2=C   R(17)→8=I   K(10)→15=P   S(18)→7=H   V(21)→4=E   I(8)→17=R
C I P H E R ✓
Answer: LEARN THE CIPHER
Teaching note: watch the midpoint pair M(12)↔N(13) — students often stumble here. Spot-check: M+N = 12+13 = 25 ✓

Homework 1 — Caesar (C) · Problem

No shift given — students must find it.
Ciphertext
XLAWP HTYD DELEP
  • Three words: 5 letters · 4 letters · 5 letters

Homework 1 — Solution

Method A — guess from a short word
4-letter word HTYD. Try common 4-letter words. Attempt WINS:
H = cipher, W = guessed plain. Shift = H(7) − W(22) = −15 ≡ 11 (mod 26)
Method B — guess from the last word
5-letter word DELEP ending in P. Attempt STATE:
D = cipher, S = guessed plain. Shift = D(3) − S(18) = −15 ≡ 11 (mod 26)
Verify shift = 11: subtract 11 from each letter (add 26 if result < 0)
X(23)−11=12=M   L(11)−11=0=A   A(0)−11=15=P   W(22)−11=11=L   P(15)−11=4=E → MAPLE ✓
H(7)−11=22=W   T(19)−11=8=I   Y(24)−11=13=N   D(3)−11=18=S → WINS ✓
D(3)−11=18=S   E(4)−11=19=T   L(11)−11=0=A   E(4)−11=19=T   P(15)−11=4=E → STATE ✓
Answer: MAPLE WINS STATE  ·  Shift = 11
Teaching note: shift 11 isn't in the "common" list (3, 13). Students who only try those will get stuck — reinforce systematic brute-force or word-guessing from short words.

Homework 2 — Atbash · Problem

Standard Atbash — resource sheet always provided.
Ciphertext
XLWVYFHGVIH MVEVI TREV FK
  • Four words: 11 letters · 5 letters · 4 letters · 2 letters

Homework 2 — Solution

Assumption: Atbash maps each letter to position 25 − (its position). Apply to every letter.
Word 1 — XLWVYFHGVIH (11 letters)
X(23)→2=C   L(11)→14=O   W(22)→3=D   V(21)→4=E   Y(24)→1=B
F(5)→20=U   H(7)→18=S   G(6)→19=T   V(21)→4=E   I(8)→17=R   H(7)→18=S
C O D E B U S T E R S ✓
Word 2 — MVEVI
M(12)→13=N   V(21)→4=E   E(4)→21=V   V(21)→4=E   I(8)→17=R → NEVER ✓
Word 3 — TREV
T(19)→6=G   R(17)→8=I   E(4)→21=V   V(21)→4=E → GIVE ✓
Word 4 — FK
F(5)→20=U   K(10)→15=P → UP ✓
Answer: CODEBUSTERS NEVER GIVE UP
Teaching note: students may forget K↔P and G↔T — these are less intuitive than A↔Z. Encourage them to write out the full mapping before starting, not just rely on memory.

Homework 3 — Caesar (C) #2 · Problem

No shift given — students must find it.
Ciphertext
YAJLCRLN FRWB
  • Two words: 8 letters · 4 letters

Homework 3 — Solution

Method — guess from the 4-letter word
FRWB: 4 letters. Try WINS: F(5) = W(22) + shift → shift = 5 − 22 = −17 ≡ 9 (mod 26)
Verify shift = 9: subtract 9 from each letter (add 26 if result < 0)
Y(24)−9=15=P   A(0)−9=17=R   J(9)−9=0=A   L(11)−9=2=C   C(2)−9=19=T   R(17)−9=8=I   L(11)−9=2=C   N(13)−9=4=E → PRACTICE ✓
F(5)−9=22=W   R(17)−9=8=I   W(22)−9=13=N   B(1)−9=18=S → WINS ✓
Answer: PRACTICE WINS  ·  Shift = 9
Teaching note: shift 9 won't be found by trying only 3 or 13. Students who brute-force systematically (or make a smart guess from a common word) will reach it in at most a few tries.

Homework 4 — Atbash #2 · Problem

Standard Atbash — resource sheet always provided.
Ciphertext
PMLDOVWTV RH KLDVI
  • Three words: 9 letters · 2 letters · 5 letters

Homework 4 — Solution

Assumption: Atbash maps each letter to position 25 − (its position). Apply to every letter.
Word 1 — PMLDOVWTV (9 letters)
P(15)→10=K   M(12)→13=N   L(11)→14=O   D(3)→22=W   O(14)→11=L
V(21)→4=E   W(22)→3=D   T(19)→6=G   V(21)→4=E
K N O W L E D G E ✓
Word 2 — RH
R(17)→8=I   H(7)→18=S → IS ✓
Word 3 — KLDVI
K(10)→15=P   L(11)→14=O   D(3)→22=W   V(21)→4=E   I(8)→17=R → POWER ✓
Answer: KNOWLEDGE IS POWER
Teaching note: W↔D is an easy pair to miss in a long word. Have students write the full plain alphabet and mirror it before decoding rather than looking up each letter individually.
